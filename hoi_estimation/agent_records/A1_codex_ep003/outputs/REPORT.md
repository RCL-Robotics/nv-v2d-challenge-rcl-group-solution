# Object trajectory refinement

Output: `object_poses_world.npz`, key `T_world_mesh`, shape `(781, 4, 4)`, float64. These are mesh-file-to-world transforms for every frame in the released world frame. No alignment, symmetry equivalence, or change to the released camera transform is applied.

The supplied reconstruction rendered the mug upside down, often with the handle on the opposite side, and drifted on the table. I replaced its orientations and re-estimated metric translation by fitting the exact textured mesh to the video and SAM2 masks. The old translations provide starting depths only; their scale correction is not retained as a constraint. A weak inclination prior uses the two intermediate tracker axes only when they agree with each other, the mask axis, and the visible-base cue; their yaw and depth are not trusted. No ground truth, external data, network requests, model weights, or later reference humans were used. Inputs were only read.

The script searches 16 azimuths at 85 keyframes, with two initial inclinations during motion. Its objective combines antialiased silhouettes, distances to foreground, robust RGB residuals with affine illumination compensation, and soft physical constraints. Pixels covered by the person are excluded from background penalties. A temporal dynamic program chooses a continuous rotation branch. All 781 frames then receive individual image refinement. Rotation is projected onto SO(3); translation is lightly smoothed in image-ray/depth coordinates, and rotation is smoothed before the stationary intervals are blended in.

Rest intervals are detected from mask bottom height: 0–135, 206–275, 341–410, 473–535, 596–645, 682–780, inclusive. Each receives a shared pose fit over interior frames. A joint fit of all six placements was accepted: their bottom normals and support plane are shared, with separate azimuths and positions. The common plane is inferred from the mug and is not the floor. The mesh bottom-face normal is estimated automatically by robust planar fitting; it is slightly oblique to the mesh z-axis. The released ground normal is a soft orientation prior, not an imposed object height.

Explicit asset/video observations are documented as constants and criteria in `solve.py`: mesh +z points toward the base, mesh +x toward the handle; the base remains below the lid in the image; the handle stays to image-left; appreciably tilted lifts expose the base/barcode toward the camera. The last observation is applied with a soft 10–16 degree mask-axis gate only when the mug is raised. These prevent axial, handle-side, and forward/backward tilt ambiguities from exploiting hand occlusion. No frame-by-frame poses were entered manually.

Validation: mean occlusion-aware silhouette IoU changed from 0.4799 to 0.9336; median changed from 0.4923 to 0.9387. This is a consistency measure against the provided masks, not ground-truth ADD. `pose_preview.jpg` shows sampled image overlays and textured renders; `silhouette_metrics.npz` contains per-frame comparisons. Finiteness, frame coverage, float64 storage, homogeneous rows, rotation orthogonality, and positive unit determinants are checked before saving.

Confidence is high in correcting the longitudinal flip, handle side, and static jitter; moderate in metric depth and inclination during motion. Remaining uncertainty comes from the small image size (roughly 30 by 70 pixels), hand occlusion, specular metal, motion blur, and estimated intrinsics/extrinsics. The physical constraints are soft and image evidence is still imperfect. No ground-truth accuracy is claimed.
